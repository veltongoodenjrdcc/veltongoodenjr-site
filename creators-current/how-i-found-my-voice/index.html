How I Found My Voice (And Why I'm Finally Using It)

On communication, creativity, and finally putting your voice to work.

Building a digital presence that actually connects with people starts with one thing: knowing what you actually sound like. Not the version of yourself you perform for different rooms. The real one, the voice that carries the weight of what you actually believe and know.

Finding that voice took me a while. This is the story of how it happened.

Communication came first

Before I had the words for marketing or brand strategy, I was already obsessed with communication. As a child, I used to go through dictionaries and thesauruses for fun. Not to study for tests. Just because I wanted more words. I was drawn to the idea that language had this enormous range, that the difference between one word and another could completely change how something landed.

I paid close attention to master communicators. The way certain people could hold a room. The way a well-told story stayed with you long after the moment. I wanted to understand the mechanics of that. How does something connect?

The DVD that changed everything

I remember watching Madagascar, the 2005 animated film, and then getting into the special features on the DVD. That was a different world. Suddenly I was watching the animators and directors talk about how the film was made. The storyboards. The choices. The people behind the scenes.

Something clicked. These were people who made things. Real things that moved other people. I decided then that I wanted to do that. My first ambition was animation.

I never became an animator. But I took something more important from that DVD: the understanding that creative worlds are built by specific people making specific choices. Nothing magical about it. Just craft, intention, and iteration.

Stickmen and early storytelling

Animation was not happening yet, I was still a child, so I did the next closest thing. I started making stick figure comics. Nothing polished. Just rough panels, scrappy dialogue, and whatever story I could manage to fit on a few pages.

What surprised me was that people wanted to read them. Friends would borrow my books to find out what happened next. They were not complimenting the artwork. They were following the story. That was the first real lesson: the quality of the story matters more than the quality of the execution.

That lesson has stayed with me in every piece of work I have done since.

Writing competitions and finding the medium

At Wolmer's Boys' School, I found poetry. I entered competitions. I won the school's poetry competition in both 2017 and 2018. I tied for fourth place in the Monica Sterling Essay Competition in 2019.

Those experiences did something specific for me. They showed me that writing, particularly the kind of writing that reaches for something true and precise, was actually a natural medium for how I thought. I did not have to translate my instincts into it. It fit.

Marketing enters the chat

The move toward marketing happened partly because I was already doing the thing, just without the name for it.

I remember a moment from childhood, I must have been eight or nine, when I looked at a bag of Mother's Food Group fragrance bags and thought: this needs a better name. The product was fine. The packaging was not interesting. And even then, I had this instinct that the way something was framed changed how it was received.

I called the idea "Go Wild." I did not have the language of brand positioning or consumer insight. But I was doing something that looked a lot like both.

When I eventually discovered marketing as a formal discipline, it felt less like a new discovery and more like finally finding the name for something I had always been doing. Marketing at its best is strategic storytelling. It is the discipline of helping people understand why something matters to them. That is what I had been drawn to all along.

Why Creator's Current exists

Creator's Current is where I put the thinking out loud. Not as a finished expert with all the answers. As someone who has been in this space long enough to notice things about content, about digital presence, about what makes communication work or fail, and who finds it useful to write those observations down.

It is for creators and small brands. The people doing the real work of building something in public without a massive team or budget behind them. The people who are figuring it out as they go.

It is not some expert playbook. It is something more honest than that. I am right here with you, making it up as I go, learning from what works and what does not, and trying to share what seems genuinely useful.

Key takeaway

Voice is not volume. It is the discipline of saying something specific, in a way only you would say it. That takes time to find, but the finding is worth it.
